# Question

If a party makes a settlement offer that is rejected and the case proceeds to trial, under what circumstances might indemnity costs be awarded against the party who rejected the offer, and how do courts distinguish this from cases involving unreasonable conduct during litigation?

# Answer

### Indemnity Costs and Rejection of Settlement Offers

When a party makes a settlement offer that is subsequently rejected, and the case proceeds to trial, indemnity costs may be awarded against the rejecting party under specific circumstances. The courts generally consider the following factors when determining whether to award indemnity costs:

1. **Reasonableness of the Offer**: The court will assess whether the settlement offer was reasonable in the context of the case. If the offer was significantly more favorable than the outcome at trial, this may weigh in favor of awarding indemnity costs against the party that rejected the offer. For instance, if the trial results in a judgment that is less favorable than the settlement offer, the rejecting party may be seen as having acted unreasonably by not accepting the offer [17].

2. **Conduct of the Parties**: The behavior of the parties during the litigation process is also crucial. Courts may award indemnity costs if the rejecting party's conduct is deemed to have unnecessarily prolonged the proceedings or if they made groundless contentions that led to increased costs for the other party. For example, in cases where a party persistently breaches court directions or engages in conduct that complicates the litigation unnecessarily, indemnity costs may be justified [18].

3. **Judicial Discretion**: The awarding of costs, including indemnity costs, is ultimately at the discretion of the court. The court will consider the overall conduct of the litigation and the specific circumstances surrounding the rejection of the settlement offer. If the rejection is viewed as part of a broader pattern of unreasonable behavior, this may lead to an indemnity costs order [1].

### Distinction from Unreasonable Conduct

The courts draw a distinction between rejecting a settlement offer and engaging in unreasonable conduct during litigation. While rejecting a reasonable offer may lead to indemnity costs, unreasonable conduct typically involves actions that are egregious or in bad faith, such as:

- **Groundless Contentions**: If a party raises claims or defenses that have no basis in law or fact, this may be considered unreasonable conduct that warrants indemnity costs [17].
- **Failure to Comply with Court Directions**: Persistent non-compliance with court orders can lead to increased costs for the opposing party, justifying an award of indemnity costs against the non-compliant party [18].

In summary, indemnity costs may be awarded against a party that rejects a reasonable settlement offer if their conduct during the litigation is deemed unreasonable or if the rejection leads to unnecessary prolongation of the proceedings. The courts exercise discretion in these matters, balancing the need for fair litigation against the conduct of the parties involved.
