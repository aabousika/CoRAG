# Question

If a party makes a settlement offer that is rejected and the case proceeds to trial, under what circumstances might indemnity costs be awarded against the party who rejected the offer, and how do courts distinguish this from cases involving unreasonable conduct during litigation?

# Answer

### Indemnity Costs in Settlement Offers

When a party makes a settlement offer that is rejected and the case proceeds to trial, indemnity costs may be awarded against the rejecting party under certain circumstances. The courts typically consider the following factors when determining whether to grant indemnity costs:

1. **Reasonableness of the Offer**: The court will assess whether the settlement offer was reasonable in the context of the case. If the offer was significantly more favorable than the eventual outcome at trial, this may weigh in favor of awarding indemnity costs against the party who rejected the offer.

2. **Conduct of the Parties**: The behavior of the parties during litigation is crucial. If the rejecting party engaged in unreasonable conduct that prolonged the proceedings or made groundless contentions, this could lead to an award of indemnity costs. For example, in *Hughes v Western Australian Cricket Association (Inc)*, the court noted that the proceedings were unduly prolonged by the making of groundless contentions, which justified the award of indemnity costs against the party responsible for such conduct [3].

3. **Prolongation of Proceedings**: If the rejection of the settlement offer resulted in unnecessary prolongation of the litigation, the court may consider this a basis for awarding indemnity costs. In *Bowen Investments Pty Ltd v Tabcorp Holdings Ltd*, the court highlighted that the conduct of the parties, including breaches of court directions, led to increased costs that could justify an indemnity costs order [4].

### Distinction from Unreasonable Conduct

The courts distinguish cases involving indemnity costs from those involving unreasonable conduct during litigation based on the nature and impact of the conduct:

- **Indemnity Costs**: These are typically awarded when a party's rejection of a reasonable settlement offer leads to a trial outcome that is less favorable than the offer, especially if the rejecting party's conduct is deemed to have unnecessarily complicated or prolonged the litigation process.

- **Unreasonable Conduct**: This refers to actions that are not just about rejecting offers but involve a broader pattern of behavior that is obstructive or frivolous. For instance, if a party consistently breaches court directions or makes baseless claims, this may lead to a finding of unreasonable conduct, which can also justify costs orders but may not necessarily lead to indemnity costs unless it directly impacts the fairness or efficiency of the proceedings [3][4].

### Conclusion

Indemnity costs may be awarded against a party that rejects a settlement offer if the offer was reasonable, and the rejection leads to prolonged litigation or if the rejecting party engages in unreasonable conduct. Courts carefully evaluate the circumstances surrounding the offer and the conduct of the parties to determine the appropriateness of such costs. However, the specific criteria and outcomes can vary based on the details of each case, and further information on the nature of the settlement offer and the trial's outcome would be beneficial for a more precise analysis.
