# Question

What are the principles for awarding indemnity costs?

# Answer

### Principles for Awarding Indemnity Costs

Indemnity costs are a specific category of cost awards in legal proceedings that differ from the standard party-party costs. The following principles govern the circumstances under which indemnity costs may be awarded:

#### 1. **Discretion of the Court**
The court has an unfettered discretion to award costs on an indemnity basis, but this discretion must be exercised judicially. The categories of cases where indemnity costs may be awarded are not closed, meaning that new circumstances can justify such an award even if they do not fit neatly into established categories [1][2][3].

#### 2. **Special or Unusual Circumstances**
Indemnity costs are typically awarded when there are special or unusual circumstances that warrant a departure from the usual party-party costs. This principle is well established in case law, particularly in the judgment of Sheppard J in *Colgate-Palmolive Company v Cussons Pty Ltd* [1993] [9][10]. Examples of such circumstances include:
- Actions or defenses maintained without a proper foundation [5][6].
- Cases where a party has acted in bad faith or has pursued claims that they knew were false [7][8].

#### 3. **Purpose of Indemnity Costs**
The primary purpose of awarding indemnity costs is to fully compensate the successful party for the costs incurred, rather than to punish the unsuccessful party. This principle is reinforced by various rulings, including *Hamod v New South Wales* [2002], which states that indemnity costs are not meant to deter litigants from pursuing uncertain claims but to ensure fair compensation for incurred costs [15][18].

#### 4. **Examples of Appropriate Cases**
Indemnity costs may be appropriate in several scenarios, including:
- Where a proceeding has been commenced without any reasonable prospects of success, particularly when the party should have known better [16][17].
- In cases of contempt, where the court finds that justice requires the respondent to pay the applicant's costs on an indemnity basis [12].

#### 5. **Judicial Considerations**
When considering whether to award indemnity costs, courts may look at factors such as:
- The conduct of the parties during the proceedings, including any undue prolongation of the case [9][10].
- Whether the issues raised were without factual basis or pursued falsely [7][8].

### Conclusion
In summary, the principles for awarding indemnity costs revolve around the court's discretion, the presence of special or unusual circumstances, and the overarching goal of compensating the successful party. The application of these principles can vary based on the specifics of each case, and courts have established a framework through various rulings to guide these decisions. However, there remains a need for clarity in certain situations, particularly regarding what constitutes "special or unusual circumstances," as interpretations can differ among cases.
