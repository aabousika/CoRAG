# Question

If a party makes a settlement offer that is rejected and the case proceeds to trial, under what circumstances might indemnity costs be awarded against the party who rejected the offer, and how do courts distinguish this from cases involving unreasonable conduct during litigation?

# Answer

### Indemnity Costs in Settlement Offers

Indemnity costs may be awarded against a party that rejects a settlement offer if the court finds that the rejection was unreasonable and that it subjected the other party to unnecessary costs. The key factors that courts consider in determining whether to award indemnity costs include:

1. **Reasonableness of the Rejection**: If the court concludes that the rejecting party's position was untenable or that they had no reasonable prospect of success at trial, indemnity costs may be awarded. For instance, in *Dukemaster Pty Ltd v Bluehive Pty Ltd*, the court noted that the applicants had limited knowledge of the respondent's case at the time of rejecting the offer, which influenced the decision against awarding indemnity costs [20].

2. **Prolongation of Proceedings**: Courts may also consider whether the rejection of the offer led to an undue prolongation of the proceedings. If the rejecting party's actions are seen as contributing to unnecessary delays or complications, this can support an indemnity costs order. In *Hughes v Western Australian Cricket Association*, the court found that groundless contentions and breaches of court directions by the respondents led to increased costs for the other party, justifying indemnity costs [2].

3. **Conduct During Litigation**: The overall conduct of the parties during litigation plays a significant role. If one party engages in unreasonable behavior, such as making groundless claims or failing to comply with court directions, this can lead to an award of indemnity costs against them. The court's focus is on whether the conduct was unreasonable enough to warrant such an award, as seen in *Hamod v New South Wales*, where costs were awarded to compensate for unreasonable litigation conduct [6].

### Distinction from Unreasonable Conduct

Courts distinguish between the rejection of settlement offers and unreasonable conduct during litigation based on the context and implications of each action:

- **Settlement Offers**: The rejection of a settlement offer alone does not automatically lead to indemnity costs. It must be shown that the rejection was unreasonable in light of the circumstances known to both parties at that time. The court will assess whether the rejecting party had a reasonable basis for their decision, considering the strength of the case and the potential outcomes [20].

- **Unreasonable Conduct**: Unreasonable conduct during litigation encompasses actions that are not merely about rejecting offers but involve broader behaviors that unnecessarily escalate costs or complicate proceedings. This includes making frivolous claims, failing to adhere to court orders, or engaging in tactics that prolong litigation without justification [2][6].

### Conclusion

Indemnity costs can be awarded against a party that rejects a settlement offer if the rejection is deemed unreasonable and leads to unnecessary costs for the other party. Courts assess the reasonableness of the rejection, the conduct of the parties, and whether the litigation was unduly prolonged. The distinction between rejecting offers and engaging in unreasonable conduct lies in the context and implications of each action, with a focus on the reasonableness of the parties' decisions and behaviors throughout the litigation process.
